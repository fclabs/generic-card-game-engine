# Pinochle (Four-Player Partnership Auction Pinochle)

A trick-taking and melding game for four players in two partnerships, played with a 48-card double deck. Players bid on how many points their side will make from meld and tricks, then the declarer names trump.

This document describes the most common version, double-deck partnership auction pinochle with passing. The other forms are in Appendix A. Point values use the traditional scale (multiples of 10).

## 1. Components

- **Deck:** 48 cards. Each of the four suits has two copies of A, 10, K, Q, J and 9. The standard 2 to 8 are removed.
- **Rank within a suit (high to low):** A, 10, K, Q, J, 9. The ten ranks above the king.
- **Players:** 4, in two fixed partnerships. Partners sit opposite each other.
- **Direction:** Play, deal and bidding all move clockwise.
- **Card points ("counters"):**

| Card | Points |
|------|--------|
| Ace | 11 |
| Ten | 10 |
| King | 4 |
| Queen | 3 |
| Jack | 2 |
| Nine | 0 |

- The last trick is worth 10 points. The deck holds 240 card points, so a hand has 250 trick points in total.
- **Identical cards:** When two identical cards (same suit and rank) are played to the same trick, the one played first outranks the second.

## 2. Objective

Score points by melding and by taking tricks with counters. A partnership wins when it reaches the target score, normally **1500 points**, before the other side does.

## 3. Setup and Dealing

1. Choose a first dealer by any method. The deal passes clockwise after each hand.
2. The dealer shuffles and the player to the dealer's right cuts.
3. The dealer deals all 48 cards clockwise, starting with the player to the dealer's left. The deal is in packets of 3 or 4 (or one at a time, depending on the table). Each player gets 12 cards.
4. **Misdeal (optional):** A player who is dealt 5 or more nines may call for a redeal.

## 4. Bidding

1. The player to the dealer's left bids first. Bidding goes clockwise.
2. Each player in turn either passes or bids higher than the previous bid. Bids are multiples of 10 and the **minimum opening bid is 250**.
3. A player who passes is out of the auction and cannot bid again.
4. Bidding ends when all but one player have passed. That player is the **declarer** (the "bidder"). Partners may not bid in a way that conveys information. Only the numeric bid is allowed.
5. **Dealer must bid:** If everyone else passes, the dealer must take the bid at the minimum, 250. This is sometimes called "stuck".
6. The bid is a promise that the declarer's side will score at least that many points in meld plus counters on the hand.
7. The declarer names the **trump suit**.

### Passing cards

After trump is named and before any meld is shown:

1. The declarer's **partner** chooses 3 cards from their hand and passes them face down to the declarer.
2. The declarer then chooses any 3 cards from their own hand, which may include any of the cards just received, and passes them face down to the partner.
3. Cards passed to the declarer may not be returned in a way that breaks the rule above. The exchange is always 3 for 3.

## 5. Meld and Play

### Melding

1. After the passing, each player lays their meld face up on the table. The declaring side should declare first.
2. Each player scores their own meld, and the partners' melds are added together as the side's meld score.
3. Meld is counted per partnership, not per hand.
4. After scoring, the cards are returned to the players' hands and are used in play.

### Meld values

Cards can count in one meld of each type. A card may be used in more than one meld if the melds are of different kinds, but not twice in the same kind. A trump marriage cannot also be part of a run.

| Meld | Cards | Points |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Class A: Trump melds** | | |
| Nine of trump ("dix") | 9 of trump | 10 each |
| Royal marriage | K and Q of trump | 40 |
| Run (flush) | A, 10, K, Q, J of trump | 150 |
| Double run | Both sets of A, 10, K, Q, J of trump | 1500 |
| **Class B: Marriages** | | |
| Common marriage | K and Q of one non-trump suit | 20 each suit |
| **Class C: Pinochle** | | |
| Pinochle | J of diamonds and Q of spades | 40 |
| Double pinochle | Both J of diamonds and both Q of spades | 300 |
| **Class D: Around** | | |
| Aces around | One A of each suit | 100 |
| Kings around | One K of each suit | 80 |
| Queens around | One Q of each suit | 60 |
| Jacks around | One J of each suit | 40 |
| 1000 aces (aces abound) | All 8 aces | 1000 |
| 800 kings (kings abound) | All 8 kings | 800 |
| 600 queens (queens abound) | All 8 queens | 600 |
| 400 jacks (jacks abound) | All 8 jacks | 400 |

Notes:
- A single card may be counted in several melds of different kinds. For example, a king can be in a marriage and in kings around.
- Several marriages in different suits are each scored separately.

### Playing the tricks

1. The declarer leads to the first trick. After that, the winner of each trick leads the next.
2. Each player plays one card to each trick, clockwise, for 12 tricks.
3. **Following suit:** A player must follow the suit led if able.
4. **Beating the trick:** A player who follows suit must play a higher card than any card already in the trick if able (see 8 for the case where a trump has been played).
5. **Void in the suit led:** A player who cannot follow suit must play a trump. If a trump is already in the trick, they must beat the highest trump if able.
6. A player who has no card of the suit led and no trump may play any card.
7. **Winning the trick:** The highest trump wins if any trump was played. Otherwise, the highest card of the suit led wins. If two identical top cards tie, the one played first wins.
8. The winner of the trick takes it in and leads next. The winner of the twelfth trick scores the 10-point last-trick bonus.

## 6. Special Terms

- **Counters:** A, 10 and K (and the other point cards) taken in tricks.
- **Declarer or bidder:** The player who won the auction.
- **Going set:** Failing to make the bid.
- **Run or flush:** A-10-K-Q-J in trump.
- **Around:** One of each suit of a given rank. **Abound:** both of each suit of a given rank.
- **Dix (pronounced "deece"):** The nine of trump.
- **Roundhouse (roundtable):** A marriage in every suit (see Appendix A). It is not counted separately in the main rule set, though its marriages score as usual.

## 7. Scoring and End of Game

### Hand scoring

1. Each side adds up its meld points and its trick points (counters taken plus 10 for the last trick).
2. **Declaring side:** The bidding side's total (meld plus trick points) must be at least the bid. If it is, the side adds that total to its game score.
3. **If the declaring side fails** (goes set), it **loses the amount of the bid** from its game score. It scores neither meld nor trick points, and its score may become negative.
4. **Defending side:** The side that did not bid scores its own meld and trick points as normal in every case.
5. Counters are rounded as given, so no rounding is needed on this scale.

### Winning

- The first side to reach **1500 points** wins.
- If both sides pass 1500 on the same hand, the **bidding side wins**, because the bidder's score is counted first.
- Play continues for as many hands as it takes.

## 8. Edge Cases and Clarifications

- **Trump already in the trick:** A player void in the suit led who holds trump must play a trump, and must overtrump a trump already played if able. If the player has trump but cannot beat the existing trump, they must still play a trump (any trump).
- **Trump led:** Every player must follow with trump and must beat the highest trump so far when able.
- **Identical cards:** If a player plays the same card as one already in the trick, the first one played wins over the second.
- **All pass:** The dealer is forced to bid the minimum of 250.
- **Last trick:** It is worth 10 points even if the trick contains no counters.
- **Meld display errors:** A player may not add cards to meld after the first lead. Any meld not shown before the first card of the first trick cannot be scored.
- **Negative scores:** A side may go below zero from repeated sets.
- **Declarer's pass-back:** The declarer may pass back cards that were just received.
- **Scoring check:** The two sides' counters always total 250 (240 in cards and 10 for the last trick).

## Appendix A: Rule Variations

Differences from the main body are noted for each.

### Scoring scale

- **Divided-by-ten scale ("modern"):** All meld values are divided by 10 (a run is 15, a marriage is 2 or 4, aces around is 10, and so on). Card points then become A/10/K = 1 each, with 1 for the last trick. The totals are the same. The minimum bid is 25 and the game target 150.
- **Simplified counters:** A, 10, K are 10 each (or 1 each in the divided scale) and Q, J, 9 are 0. Alternatively A and 10 are 10 each and K and Q are 5 each. The main body uses 11/10/4/3/2/0.

### Bid and target

- **Minimum bid:** Some sources use 150 and some 200 for the opening bid, and others set the minimum at 20 or 25 on the divided scale. The main body uses 250.
- **Game target:** 1000 points and 1500 points are both common. Some sources play to 150 on the divided scale. The main body uses 1500.
- **Bid increments:** Some tables allow increments of 10 only, and others make the increment 5 or require a raise of at least 10. The main body uses multiples of 10.
- **Dealer's bid:** In some versions the dealer, if all pass, is forced to bid. Others allow a redeal instead.

### Meld and play

- **Meld requires taking a trick:** In some rules (Trickster options, and the three-handed game), a declaring side that takes no counters scores nothing, even if it makes the bid. The main body does not have this rule.
- **No passing cards:** Some games omit the 3-card pass, or use 2 or 4 cards (Trickster options).
- **Kitty variant:** Four-player game with 11 cards each and 4 cards in a kitty that the declarer picks up and then discards from.
- **Roundhouse:** K and Q in each suit scores 240 (counting the four marriages as 80 + 20 + 20 + 20 plus the extra), sometimes scored as 240 on its own.
- **Heading rule:** In some post-1945 rules, a player must beat the trick only if trump was led, or does not have to head the trick at all. The main body requires heading the trick.
- **Shared cards:** Some rules do not allow a card in a run to also be in a royal marriage, or in a double run.
- **Misdeal:** Some tables call a misdeal for 5 nines, others for a hand with no counters.

### Other forms of the game

- **Three-handed (cutthroat) auction pinochle:** Each player has 15 cards and 3 go to a kitty. The player to the dealer's left bids first, with a minimum of 300 (Pagat). The declarer takes the kitty, discards 3, and names trump. The declarer alone melds and plays against the other two. Settlement is made per hand in units, with a payment table by bid, doubled when spades are trump, and no fixed target.
- **Two-handed pinochle:** 12 cards each in packets of 4, leaving a 24-card stock. Players draw after each trick and score one meld per trick won in the first 12 tricks. Melded cards stay on the table and can be played. In the final 12 tricks, melded cards return to hand and normal trick-taking rules apply.
- **Single-deck pinochle (Bezique relatives):** Not covered here.
