# Canasta (Classic, 4-player partnership)

Canasta is a Rummy-family partnership game in which players meld sets of same-rank cards, aim to complete seven-card "canastas", and race to a cumulative score of 5,000. The main body describes **Classic Canasta** (the original 1940s rules, the consensus reference). Modern American Canasta and other variants are in Appendix A.

## 1. Components

- **Deck**: two standard 52-card packs plus four jokers (108 cards).
- **Players**: 4, in two fixed partnerships, partners seated opposite. (2, 3 and 6 player rules: Appendix A.)
- Cards are used as follows:
  - **Natural cards**: A, K, Q, J, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4. Only these can form the base of a meld.
  - **Wild cards**: all 2s (deuces) and jokers.
  - **Red threes** (3 of hearts/diamonds): bonus cards.
  - **Black threes** (3 of clubs/spades): "stop" cards, and the only cards that may be melded as a special going-out set.
- **Card point values**: Joker 50; A and 2: 20; K, Q, J, 10, 9, 8: 10; 7, 6, 5, 4: 5; black 3: 5. Red threes are 100 each as bonuses (see scoring).

## 2. Objective

Score points by melding cards, making canastas, collecting red threes, and going out. The first partnership to reach a cumulative 5,000 or more points at the end of a hand wins the game.

## 3. Setup and Dealing

1. Partnerships are determined by drawing cards: the two highest are partners against the two lowest. The highest drawer picks seats and the dealer is chosen accordingly (deal then rotates to the left each hand).
2. Dealer deals **11 cards** to each player, one at a time, clockwise, starting with the player on the dealer's left.
3. The remaining cards form the face-down **stock**. Turn the top card face up to start the **discard pile**. If this starter card is a wild card (2 or joker) or a red three, turn further cards until a natural card appears; the extra cards stay in the pile. The pile starts **frozen** in these cases (turn the wild/red-three cards sideways to mark it). (Bicycle also skips a starting black three; Pagat does not mention it.)
4. **Red threes in the initial hand**: each player lays any red threes face up in front of them and draws replacements from the stock (repeat if a replacement is also a red three).

## 4. Turn Structure

Play proceeds clockwise starting from the dealer's left. A turn is:

1. **Draw** (mandatory), either:
   - take the top card of the stock; or
   - take the whole **discard pile** if legally able (section 4.3).
2. **Meld** (optional): lay down melds, add cards to your side's existing melds, and/or make a canasta.
3. **Discard** one card face up on the pile. This ends the turn (unless the player went out).

### 4.1 Red threes

Whenever a red three is drawn from the stock it must be laid face up immediately, and a replacement drawn from the stock (it does not count as a meld). A red three cannot be taken in the discard pile; a red three is never discarded.

### 4.2 Melds

- A meld is **three or more cards of the same rank**, containing **at least two natural cards** and **at most three wild cards**.
- Wild cards may never form a meld of their own and cannot represent threes.
- A partnership may have only **one meld per rank**: further cards of that rank are added to the existing meld (by either partner). Melds belong to the partnership and are laid in front of whoever made them, but both partners may add to any of them.
- A meld of seven or more cards is a **canasta**. Natural (pure) canasta: no wild cards. Mixed (dirty) canasta: contains wild cards (up to three). Convention: a pure canasta is squared up with a red card on top; a mixed canasta with a black card on top.
- A meld may grow beyond seven cards, but never above three wild cards in total; a pure canasta becomes mixed if a wild card is added (the three-wild limit still applies).
- Black threes can only be melded together (3 or 4, no wild cards) on the turn a player goes out (see 4.5).

### 4.2.1 Initial meld minimum

A partnership's first meld of the hand must reach a minimum **point count** (sum of card values of the cards laid down), based on the partnership's score at the start of the hand:

| Cumulative score | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Negative | 15 |
| 0 to 1,495 | 50 |
| 1,500 to 2,995 | 90 |
| 3,000 or more | 120 |

- Several melds may be combined in the same turn to reach the minimum.
- Bonus points (canastas, red threes) do **not** count toward the minimum.
- If the initial meld is made by taking the discard pile, the top card (with the hand cards used with it) counts toward the minimum; other cards of the pile may be melded afterwards but do not count toward the minimum.
- After a partnership's initial meld, either partner may make any valid meld with no minimum.
- A player may go out on their first meld without meeting the minimum if they go out concealed (4.5).

### 4.3 Taking the discard pile

The player takes the **entire pile** (all cards), then may meld and must discard.

**Never allowed** if the top card is a wild card, a red three, or a black three.

**Unfrozen pile** (the pile is not frozen to that player): the player may take it if the top card can be:
- added to one of their side's existing melds of that rank; or
- melded with **two natural cards** of the same rank from hand; or
- melded with **one natural and one wild card** from hand (per Wikipedia/Bicycle; Pagat notes some play disallow this, see A.5).

**Frozen pile**: may be taken only by melding the top card with **two natural cards of the same rank** from hand (not wild cards, and not adding to an existing meld).

The pile is **frozen** for everyone when:
- a wild card is discarded onto it (turn it sideways; the pile stays frozen until taken); or
- a red three was part of the starter card(s).

The pile is also frozen **for a partnership that has not yet made its initial meld** (it must use two natural cards from hand for the top card).

Black three: discarding a black three prevents the next player from taking the pile (the next player only; it does not freeze it for later players).

### 4.4 Stock exhaustion

- If the last card of the stock is drawn and is a red three, it is laid down and the hand ends (no replacement).
- When the stock is empty, play continues only if the next player takes the discard pile (when legally able); otherwise the hand ends. (Whether taking is compulsory is not stated in the consulted text.)

### 4.5 Going out

- A player may go out only if their partnership has **completed at least one canasta** (or they complete one in going out).
- Going out: meld all cards, or meld all but one and discard the last card. Going out earns the **going out bonus**. A player may ask "Partner, may I go out?" before going out; the partner's answer is binding (partner may answer yes or no).
- **Going out concealed**: the player melds their **whole hand in one turn** (including at least one canasta) without having previously melded and without adding to partner's melds. Earns the concealed bonus instead of the normal one. The initial meld minimum does not apply in this case.
- A player who has no canasta must keep a card after discarding (must not discard their last card, i.e. cannot go out without a canasta).
- When going out, a player may meld 3 or 4 black threes as a set (no wild cards).
- The hand ends immediately when a player goes out.

## 5. Special Cards and Terms

- **Wild cards**: 2s and jokers; substitute for any natural rank (not threes).
- **Red three**: 100-point bonus each (800 for all four) if the side has melded; a penalty if the side has not melded.
- **Black three**: stop card; worth 5 points; only meldable on going out.
- **Frozen pile**: pile that can only be taken with a natural pair.
- **Canasta**: meld of 7+ cards. **Natural/pure**: no wilds. **Mixed/dirty**: has wilds.
- **Concealed**: going out with the entire hand in one turn.
- **Stock**: face-down draw pile. **Upcard**: top card of the discard pile.

## 6. Scoring and End of Game

At the end of a hand each partnership scores:

**Bonuses (added):**
- Going out: 100
- Going out concealed: 200 total (100 extra)
- Each natural canasta: 500
- Each mixed canasta: 300
- Red threes: 100 each; all four red threes: 800 total

**Card values (added for melded, subtracted for cards left in hand):** use the values in section 1. Melded card values count for the partnership; cards left in both partners' hands count against it.

**Red three penalty**: if a partnership has red threes but has **not** made an initial meld when the hand ends, each red three counts **minus 100** (all four: minus 800).

The score is added to (or subtracted from) the cumulative total. The game ends when a partnership has **5,000 or more** at the end of a hand; the final hand is always played out. The higher total wins (if both exceed 5,000, higher total wins).

## 7. Edge Cases and Clarifications

- Bonuses for red threes and canastas never count toward the initial meld minimum.
- A partnership never has two melds of the same rank; extra cards of that rank join the existing meld.
- The initial meld minimum is determined by the partnership score at the start of the hand.
- The pile cannot be taken when its top card is a wild card, red three or black three.
- A black three discard blocks only the next player and does not freeze the pile; a wild card discard freezes it until it is taken.
- Taking the pile transfers all its cards to the taker's hand.
- A side that has not yet melded treats the pile as frozen.
- A side without a canasta cannot go out and must retain a card after discarding.
- Red threes are never part of a meld and are never discarded.

## Appendix A: Rule Variations

**A.1 Modern American Canasta (differs substantially)**
- 13 cards each; a "turn card" is the ninth card from the bottom of the draw pile (draw pile has no initial discard).
- Target 8,500. Initial meld minimums: 125 (below 3,000), 155 (3,000-4,995), 180 (5,000+).
- Three ways to initial meld: minimum count, "splash" (natural or wild canasta in hand), or "special hand" (straight, seven pairs, garbage).
- Pile can be taken only with a natural pair; threes cannot be discarded; wild cards only discardable when going out.
- Aces and sevens melds must be pure or have special rules; incomplete aces/sevens/wild melds are penalised.
- Two canastas required to go out; cannot go out by melding the whole hand (must discard last card).
- Canasta bonuses: mixed 300, pure 500, pure aces/sevens 2,500, wild canasta 2,000-3,000.
- Threes score only if the team has two canastas.

**A.2 Two players**: 15 cards each; draw two cards per turn, discard one; two canastas needed to go out; 5,000 target.

**A.3 Three players**: 13 cards (some play 11); draw two, discard one; first player to take the pile plays alone against the other two as a temporary partnership; red threes scored individually; 7,500 target.

**A.4 Six players**: three 52-card decks plus six jokers; 13 cards each; two canastas to go out; teams of two (three teams, 7,500) or teams of three (two teams, 10,000); red three bonus 400 for four, 1,000 for five, 1,200 for six.

**A.5 Other classic differences**
- Jokers: some play with 2 jokers per pack (4 total), some with 3 (110 cards) per Wikipedia.
- Some play the pile may be taken with one natural and one wild (unfrozen), others require a natural pair always (always frozen).
- Wikipedia lists end-of-hand penalty for unmelded red threes as 500 (other sources: 100).
- Some play allows wild-card-only melds (2,000 bonus).
- **Viennese Canasta**: red threes count only if a canasta is melded; wild cards may not outnumber naturals; discarding a card that fits an opponent's canasta blocks the pile like a black three.
- **Canadian**: three decks, 15 cards each, "prize pile" of five cards, draw two cards, target 10,000, two red canastas and a wild canasta to go out.
- **Samba**: three decks; sequences allowed; sambas worth 1,500; target 10,000.
- **Bolivia**: three decks; target 15,000; wild canasta 2,500.
- **Hand and Foot**: four or more decks; each player has a hand and a foot; red threes -300.
