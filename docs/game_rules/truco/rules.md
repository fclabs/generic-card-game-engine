# Truco (Argentine) - Rules

Truco is a bluffing-heavy trick-taking game for 2, 4 or 6 players, played with a 40-card Spanish deck. Players bet on tricks (Truco), on card-pair points (Envido) and on three-of-a-suit hands (Flor), and the first side to reach 15 or 30 points wins.

## 1. Components

- **Deck:** 40-card Spanish deck. Suits: Espadas (swords), Bastos (clubs/batons), Oros (coins/gold), Copas (cups). Ranks: 1 (As/Ancho), 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10 (Sota, jack), 11 (Caballo, knight), 12 (Rey, king). There are no 8s, 9s or jokers.
- **Players:** 2 (head to head), 4 (two partnerships, partners sit opposite) or 6 (two teams of 3, seating alternates). Play and dealing rotate counter-clockwise (to the right).
- **Scoring aid:** score is kept with matches/beans/paper marks. It is split into "malas" and "buenas" (see section 7).

### Card hierarchy (trick strength, highest to lowest)

| Rank | Card | Common name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 of Espadas | Ancho de espadas / Macho |
| 2 | 1 of Bastos | Ancho de bastos / Hembra |
| 3 | 7 of Espadas | Siete bravo / Siete de espadas |
| 4 | 7 of Oros | Siete bravo / Siete de oros |
| 5 | All 3s | Tres |
| 6 | All 2s | Dos |
| 7 | 1 of Copas, 1 of Oros | Falsos ases (false aces) |
| 8 | 12 (Rey) | Rey |
| 9 | 11 (Caballo) | Caballo |
| 10 | 10 (Sota) | Sota |
| 11 | 7 of Copas, 7 of Bastos | Falsos sietes (false sevens) |
| 12 | All 6s | Seis |
| 13 | All 5s | Cinco |
| 14 | All 4s | Cuatro |

Cards of equal rank (e.g. two 3s; 12s of different suits) are tied in trick strength; suit does not break the tie.

### Envido point values of cards

| Card | Envido value |
|---|---|
| 1 to 7 | face value |
| 10, 11, 12 | 0 |

(Trick strength and envido value are independent.)

## 2. Objective

Be the first side to reach the target score (15 or 30 points, agreed beforehand; 30 is the usual full game) by winning points from Truco (tricks), Envido and Flor in successive hands.

## 3. Setup and dealing

1. Choose the first dealer (any method). The dealer shuffles and offers the deck to the player on their left to cut; the cut deck is returned.
2. Deal three cards face down to each player, one at a time, starting with the player to the dealer's right.
3. The player to the dealer's right is **mano** (leads the first trick, wins ties). The dealer is the last to play; in later hands the deal and the mano role rotate one seat to the right.
4. In team games the player designated to act as mano/dealer is by seat; partners may not show cards (see signs, section 8).

## 4. Turn structure (one hand)

A hand ("mano" in the loose sense) consists of up to three tricks (**bazas / manos**) and optional betting phases.

### 4.1 Trick play

1. Mano leads any card from their hand face up; play continues to the right, each player playing one card.
2. The highest card by the hierarchy wins the trick. The winner leads the next trick. If a trick is tied (**parda**, see 4.2), the mano of the hand leads the next trick.
3. After three tricks (or earlier, once decided) the hand ends. The side winning the most tricks wins the Truco stake.

### 4.2 Winning the hand (parda rules)

Truco is decided by tricks, with these tie rules:

- Win 2 tricks: you win the hand (the third is not played).
- 1st trick tied (parda): whoever wins the 2nd trick wins the hand.
- 1st trick won by A, 2nd tied: A wins the hand.
- 1st trick won by A, 2nd won by B, 3rd tied: A (winner of 1st trick) wins the hand.
- 1st and 2nd tied: whoever wins the 3rd wins the hand.
- All three tied: the mano wins the hand.

### 4.3 Truco chain (bets on the hand)

Stakes: nothing called = **1 point**.

| Call | Meaning | If accepted | If refused ("no quiero") |
|---|---|---|---|
| Truco | "Truco" | hand worth 2 | caller gets 1 point |
| Retruco | re-raise to 3 | hand worth 3 | the Truco caller gets 2 |
| Vale cuatro | re-raise to 4 | hand worth 4 | the Retruco caller gets 3 |

Rules:
- A call may be made on your turn (commonly before or while playing a card) by any player, in team games any member of the team.
- The opponent answers **Quiero** (accept), **No quiero** (refuse; the hand ends and the caller wins the points shown above) or raises to the next level (and the Quiero is then implied for the previous level).
- Only the side that *accepted* the previous call may raise next; the same side cannot raise twice in a row. Vale cuatro is the last level.
- **Mazo** (going to the deck): a player may fold their cards at any time on their turn, conceding the hand. The opposing side wins the current accepted stake (1 if no Truco was accepted; the accepted level otherwise). In team games, a partner folding concedes for the whole team in the hand (in 2v2 it ends the hand for the team; partners may not fold only one).
- Once a Truco is accepted, Envido and Flor can no longer be called.

### 4.4 Envido (bet on card-pair points)

- Envido may be called only during the **first trick** (before it is completed) and only while no Truco has been *accepted*. If a Truco was called but not yet answered, Envido may still be called and is resolved first ("envido va primero").
- The score of a hand ("tanto"): with two or three cards of the same suit, **20 + the sum of the two highest envido values of that suit**. With no two cards of the same suit, the tanto is the single highest envido value among the three cards. Maximum is 33 (7+6 of one suit); a same-suit pair scores at least 20.
- Calls and stakes:

| Call | Points if accepted | Points if refused |
|---|---|---|
| Envido | 2 | 1 to the caller |
| Real Envido | 3 | 1 |
| Falta Envido | see below | 1 |
| Envido, Envido | 4 | 2 |
| Envido, Real Envido | 5 | 2 |
| Envido, Envido, Real Envido | 7 | 4 |
| Envido, Envido, Falta Envido / any chain ending in Falta Envido | Falta Envido value | points of the chain before Falta (1 if Falta was first) |

- The responding side can answer Quiero, No quiero, or raise. Allowed order: Envido (max twice), Real Envido (once), Falta Envido (once). A raise may not go back down (after Real Envido, only Falta Envido can follow; after Falta Envido nothing).
- Refusing a raise gives the raiser the points of the accumulated chain *before* the raise (the points already "accepted" by the refuser).
- If accepted, players state their tantos in order: mano first ("tengo N"); the opponent answers "son buenas" (yours is better), or states a higher or equal number. **The mano wins ties.** (Tradition: the player must say "son buenas" if they cannot beat it; cards need not be shown unless challenged; a player who claims a false tanto loses it.) In team games, the team's tanto is the best of its members; the mano side announces first.
- Cards played in the trick sequence may be shown on request to verify a tanto.
- **Falta Envido value:** if the winner's opponent (the side currently leading) is in the buenas, the winner gets the points that leader still needs to reach the target (e.g. 30 minus leader's score). If the leader is still in the malas (under 15 in a 30-point game), the winner gets the points that the leader still needs to reach 15, i.e. the end of the malas.

### 4.5 Flor (optional rule; commonly played in competitive/club Argentine Truco)

- A **flor** is a hand of three cards of the same suit, announced at the first opportunity (before the first card is played, or on the player's first turn). Its score is **20 + the sum of the three envido values** (max 38).
- When a player has flor, Envido may no longer be called by that player's opponent; calling Envido against a flor holder is answered with "flor" which cancels the envido bet.
- A flor held by only one side scores **3 points** automatically ("flor" declared; opponent says "con flor me achico" only if they too have flor).
- If both sides have flor, the challenged side may raise: **Contraflor** (winner 6 points) or **Contraflor al resto** (winner gets the points needed to finish the game); "con flor envido" (higher flor tanto wins; 4 points per some sources, see Appendix). Ties go to the mano. Sources differ on refusal values; see Appendix A.1.
- Flor must be called before Truco is accepted and before the first trick is finished; once a Truco has been accepted, Flor can no longer be called.
- If flor is not announced in time it cannot be claimed later (a player in a flor game who has flor and calls envido instead simply loses the flor points).

## 5. Game flow summary for an engine

```
deal(3 cards each) -> state: mano, dealer
for trick in 1..3:
    legal actions on turn: play card | envido-family (trick 1 only, no accepted truco) |
                           flor (before first card, if enabled) |
                           truco-family (if side may raise) | mazo
    resolve envido/flor immediately when called (before the pending card is played)
    after each player plays, resolve trick winner; apply parda rules for next leader
hand ends when: 2 tricks won / parda rules decide / mazo / no quiero
score: truco stake + envido + flor points (envido and flor are awarded when resolved)
check game end; rotate mano and dealer
```

## 6. Special terms

- **Mano** - player to the dealer's right; leads, wins ties (envido tanto, all-tied tricks).
- **Pie** - the dealer / last player to act.
- **Baza / mano** - a trick.
- **Parda** - tied trick.
- **Truco / Retruco / Vale cuatro** - stake escalation (2/3/4 points).
- **Envido / Real Envido / Falta Envido** - side bet on card-pair points (2 / 3 / rest-of-game).
- **Flor / Contraflor / Contraflor al resto** - three-of-a-suit bonus bets.
- **Quiero / No quiero** - accept / decline.
- **Son buenas** - "yours are good": concedes the envido to the opponent.
- **Mazo** - fold. "Irse al mazo".
- **Las malas / las buenas** - first 15 points / second 15 points of a 30-point game.
- **Cartas bravas** - the four strongest cards (1 Espadas, 1 Bastos, 7 Espadas, 7 Oros).
- **Macho / Hembra** - 1 of Espadas / 1 of Bastos.
- **Señas** - secret signals between partners (e.g. raised eyebrows = 1 Espadas, wink = 1 Bastos, mouth to the right = 7 Espadas, mouth to the left = 7 Oros, bite lip = a 3, kiss = a 2, fish mouth = false ace, close eyes = no points / bad cards).

## 7. Scoring and end of game

- Points are collected from Truco (1-4), Envido (1-7+ or Falta), and Flor (3/6/rest).
- Target: **30 points** (full game, split into 15 malas + 15 buenas) or **15 points** (short game). The first side to reach or pass the target wins immediately, even in the middle of a hand if points are awarded by a resolved bet.
- Order of scoring within a hand: Envido/Flor are resolved first (they are decided before the trick result), then Truco. If one side would reach the target through Envido, the hand ends and the Truco points are not played.
- In a 2-player or 4-player game, the mano passes to the right each hand.

## 8. Edge cases and clarifications

- Envido only exists in the first trick: if the first trick is complete (both sides played) it can no longer be called.
- A player who has already played their first card may still call Envido if the opposing side has not yet played in the first trick.
- Truco may be called before the first card is played and at any point afterwards; the Envido call takes priority and is resolved first.
- When Truco is called and the opponent raises with Retruco, the opponent's Envido right has expired only if the Truco is accepted.
- In games with Flor, if a player has flor, they cannot "bluff" envido; the points of a flor declared late are lost.
- Partners may not communicate other than through the traditional señas; spoken table talk is not allowed.
- When all players are tied in a trick with identical rank, the cards are "parda" regardless of suit.
- The point total never exceeds the target: points are capped for display, though the game ends at reaching it.
- A hand ends when the outcome is mathematically decided (e.g. after two tricks won by one side), without playing the third card.
- "Quiero" to an unresolved Falta Envido can decide the game outright; engine should check game end after every award.

## Appendix A: Rule Variations

Differences from the main (Argentine) body are labelled **[DIFF]**.

### A.1 Argentine regional/house variants
- **[DIFF] Falta Envido value:** the Mendoza (and some house rules) variant: winner takes *all remaining points* the winner needs to reach the target (some sources: the full game if no one has reached 15) instead of the points the leading opponent needs. Wikipedia ES/La Capital describe it as depending on the score position.
- **[DIFF] Flor values:** some sources: lone flor = 3; both have flor = 4 ("con flor envido" 5 in Uruguayan); contraflor = 6; contraflor al resto = points to win. Others omit Flor (called "sin flor" / "sin jardinera") entirely; in several communities Flor is optional and off by default.
- **[DIFF] Stacked Envido counting:** sources agree on Envido+Envido = 4, Envido+Real = 5, Envido+Envido+Real = 7; the refusal values (2 / 2 / 4) in the main body follow La Capital/SitioAndino; a minority give 3/3/5.
- **[DIFF] Game length:** 15 or 30 points (main = 30); some groups play to 15 only with no malas/buenas.
- **[DIFF] Pica pica / Punta y hacha (6 players):** hands alternate between "redonda" (all six play, team total) and "pica pica / punta y hacha" (three simultaneous one-vs-one duels whose scores sum), usually only once a team is in the buenas.
- **[DIFF] Gallo / Pata de gallo (3 players):** three-player free-for-all with rotating alliances; Truco points go to individual players.

### A.2 Uruguayan Truco (truco con muestra)
- **[DIFF]** After dealing, one card is turned face up (the **muestra**). The suit of the muestra defines **piezas** (special top-ranking cards): the 2, 4, 5, 10 (sota) and 11 (caballo) of that suit rank above the usual hierarchy (one source says only when the muestra is Oros, likely a misreading). Exact piece order and substitution when the muestra itself is a pieza are not verified.
- **[DIFF]** Envido with piezas: per EN Wikipedia the caballo (11) of the muestra suit is worth 30 and the sota (10) 29 for envido; a pieza combines with a card of another suit. Only partly verified.
- **[DIFF]** Target: commonly 30 points (15+15) in many sources; one source gives 40 (two segments of 20).
- **[DIFF]** Flor includes three same-suit cards or certain piece combinations; "con flor envido" is worth 5 when both have flor.
- Truco, Retruco and Vale cuatro remain 2/3/4 (1/2/3 refused).

### A.3 Brazilian Truco
- **Common to Brazilian variants:** 40-card deck (A,2,3,4,5,6,7,Q,J,K; the 10 is not used as a separate rank in the sources consulted); 2 teams of 2 (or 2 teams of 3); first to **12 points** wins; no Envido or Flor.
- **Truco Paulista [DIFF]:** order of cards (low to high): 4,5,6,7,Q,J,K,A,2,3. After the deal one card is turned (**vira**); the **manilhas** are the next rank above the vira (e.g. vira 4 -> 5s are manilhas). Manilha order: Zap (clubs) > Copas (hearts) > Espadas (spades) > Ouros (diamonds) - highest to lowest. Hand stake 1 point; Truco = 3, then Seis (6), Nove (9), Doze (12). Refused: lower stake. Tied tricks follow standard rules ("empachada"/"embuchada"). **Mao de Onze:** a team at 11 sees the partner's cards and may play for 3 or refuse (opponents get 1). **Mao de ferro** when both at 11: play blind.
- **Truco Mineiro [DIFF]:** fixed manilhas (commonly 4 of clubs, 7 of hearts, ace of spades, 7 of diamonds in descending order; verify with a Mineiro-specific source before implementing), a hand is worth **2 points**; Truco 4, then 6/10/12. **Mao de dez** at 10 points: play for 4 points or refuse for 2.
- Scoring ladder sources: Truco -> Seis -> Nove -> Doze (Paulista); some sources add "Pedir queda / pedir jogo" etc.

### A.4 Spanish (Valencian/Balearic) Truc
- **[DIFF]** Original Catalan/Valencian game; 40-card Spanish deck or a reduced deck; same top hierarchy (As of espadas "La Major", As of bastos "El Basto", 7 of espadas "La Manilla"/"Manilla", 7 of oros), partners with signs; calls: Envit (envido), Truc, Retruc (Retruque), Quatre/Val quatre, with point scales similar to Argentine Truco. Details vary strongly by region and are not further specified here.
