# 1. Euchre

A four-player partnership trick-taking game with a 24-card deck in which one suit is made trump each hand, the two jacks of the trump color become the highest cards, and the side that names trump (the "makers") must win at least 3 of 5 tricks. This file describes the North American standard.

## 2. Components

- **Players:** 4, in two fixed partnerships; partners sit opposite each other. (Seating order clockwise: A, B, A', B'.)
- **Deck:** 24 cards, A K Q J 10 9 in each of the four suits (standard pack with 2-8 removed). No joker.
- **Other:** a way to keep score (traditionally low cards, or a tally).
- **Direction:** all play, dealing and bidding go clockwise.

## 3. Objective

Be the first partnership to reach **10 points** (see Section 7). Each hand, the makers try to win at least 3 of the 5 tricks; the defenders try to stop them.

## 4. Setup and Dealing

1. Choose the first dealer by any method (e.g. first black jack in a deal-out, or random). The deal passes to the left after every hand.
2. Shuffle; the player to the dealer's right may cut.
3. The dealer deals 5 cards to each player, clockwise starting with the player to the dealer's left ("eldest hand"), in packets: 3 then 2, or 2 then 3 (each packet size is the same for all players within a round; the pattern may vary between rounds/hands).
4. The 4 undealt cards form the **kitty**. The dealer turns the top kitty card face up: the **up-card**. The rest stay face down and out of play.

## 5. Gameplay (Turn Structure)

### 5.1 Card ranking (once trump is set)

- **Trump suit (highest to lowest):** Right Bower (jack of trump), Left Bower (jack of the same-color suit), A, K, Q, 10, 9.
- **Non-trump suits:** A, K, Q, J, 10, 9. The suit that shares trump's color has only 5 cards (its jack is now trump).
- The Left Bower belongs to the trump suit for all purposes (following suit, leading, being led).

### 5.2 Making trump - round 1 (up-card suit)

Starting with eldest hand (left of dealer) and going clockwise, ending with the dealer, each player in turn either:
- **Passes**, or
- **Orders it up** (the dealer says "I pick it up"/"take it up"): the up-card's suit becomes trump.

When trump is set in round 1: the dealer adds the up-card to their hand (6 cards) and discards one card face down (it may be the up-card). The discard is out of play. The player who ordered up (or the dealer, if the dealer took it) is a **maker**; their partnership is the **makers**.

### 5.3 Making trump - round 2 (any other suit)

If all four pass in round 1, the dealer turns the up-card face down (the kitty is not used). Starting again with eldest hand, each player in turn either passes or **names a trump suit** - any suit except the suit of the rejected up-card. The namer's partnership becomes the makers. No one picks up or discards in round 2.

If all four pass again, the hand is thrown in (no score); the deal passes left.

### 5.4 Going alone (optional)

After trump is fixed and before the first lead, the maker (the player who ordered up/named, or, if the dealer's partner or opponent ordered up, only that player) may declare **alone** ("loner"). Their partner puts their cards face down and sits out the hand. (Pagat says any player may announce alone; see Appendix A.)

### 5.5 Play of the hand

1. Eldest hand (the player to the dealer's left) leads the first trick. If that player is sitting out, the next active player clockwise leads.
2. Players play one card each, clockwise. A player **must follow suit** (the effective suit, accounting for the Left Bower) if able; otherwise may play any card, including trump.
3. A trick is won by the highest trump played; if no trump, by the highest card of the led suit.
4. The trick winner leads the next trick. Play continues for 5 tricks. Tricks are kept face down in front of the winner (count per partnership).

## 6. Special Cards and Terms

- **Right Bower:** jack of trump; highest card.
- **Left Bower:** other jack of the same color; second-highest, counts as trump.
- **Up-card:** the turned kitty card that proposes trump in round 1.
- **Order up / Pick it up:** accept the up-card suit as trump in round 1.
- **Makers / Defenders:** partnership that fixed trump / the other partnership.
- **Euchre (euchred):** makers win fewer than 3 tricks.
- **March:** makers win all 5 tricks.
- **Alone / loner:** maker plays without partner.
- **Kitty:** the 4 undealt cards.

## 7. Scoring and End of Game

| Result | Points |
|---|---|
| Makers win 3 or 4 tricks | 1 to makers |
| Makers win all 5 (march) | 2 to makers |
| Makers win 1-2 or 0 tricks (euchred) | 2 to defenders |
| Lone maker wins 3 or 4 tricks | 1 to makers |
| Lone maker wins all 5 | 4 to makers |
| Lone maker euchred | 2 to defenders |

The game ends as soon as a partnership reaches **10 or more points** at the end of a hand; that partnership wins.

## 8. Edge Cases and Clarifications

- **Left Bower as trump:** if hearts are trump, the J of diamonds is a heart; leading it is leading trump, and a player holding it need not follow a diamond lead with it (a diamond lead requires following with a diamond that is not the jack of diamonds if one is held). Conversely, J of hearts in a non-trump context does not exist.
- **Dealer picks up:** the dealer's hand has 6 cards until they discard; they must discard before the first lead. Dealer may keep the up-card and discard any other card.
- **Dealer's partner going alone:** if the dealer's partner orders up and goes alone, the dealer does not pick up (the dealer sits out), though the up-card still sets trump. Common practice: the dealer discards nothing and simply sits out.
- **Lone hand with partner of the leader sitting out:** play proceeds with 3 players; the leader is the next active player clockwise from the dealer.
- **Dealer sitting out:** if the dealer is the partner of a lone maker, the dealer's hand is not used, and the deal still passes normally.
- **Misdeal:** an incorrect deal (wrong card count, exposed card) is generally redealt by the same dealer, provided it's discovered before trump is made.
- **Renege (failure to follow suit):** if discovered, the offending side usually loses the hand: the opponents score 2 points (4 if the offender was alone or opposing a lone hand per local custom). Detected only before the next trick is led; a renege found later is not penalized.
- **All pass twice:** no score, next dealer deals.
- **Tricks:** a player may look at only their own last trick if house rules allow; officially once a trick is turned, it is not inspected.

## Appendix A: Rule Variations

Differences from the main body are marked.

1. **British (Pagat "Euchre"):** 25-card pack including a joker (the "Benny" or "Best Bower"), the highest trump, above the Right Bower; game to **11** points. If the up-card is the Benny, the dealer must announce trump before picking up. (Differs: deck, game target, extra top trump.)
2. **Stick the dealer:** in round 2 the dealer may not pass and must name trump. Very common in North America. (Differs: removes the throw-in hand.)
3. **Deck sizes:** 28-card (adds 8s), 32-card (adds 8s and 7s; the original 1844 form, also in Bicycle's text). Differs from the 24-card main body.
4. **Game targets:** 5, 7, 10, or 11 points. Main body uses 10.
5. **Who may go alone:** Pagat states any player may announce alone after trump is made; main body (and most North American play) restricts it to the maker. (Sources vary.)
6. **Call for partner:** instead of going alone, a maker asks the partner to pass their best card in exchange for the maker's worst. (Regional; differs.)
7. **Farmer's hand / Ace no face:** a hand of only 9s and 10s ("farmer's hand") may be shown, forcing a misdeal or allowing the player to swap three cards with the kitty; "Ace no face" similarly may force a misdeal. (Differs; absent from main body.)
8. **Canadian / Ontario:** if the dealer's partner wants to order up the dealer, they must play alone. (Differs.)
9. **Six-player:** two teams of three, 33+ cards; march and euchre are worth 3 points, a lone march 6. (Differs.)
10. **Ohio scoring:** score kept with a 4 and a 6 card, traditionally the 4 on top of the 6. (Cosmetic.)
11. **Dealing patterns:** some sources specify only 3-2 or 2-3 per deal; Pagat allows packets of two or three in any order. (Minor.)
12. **Renege penalties and misdeal handling** vary by house; the body's treatment is an unsourced convention and should be treated as configurable in an engine.
13. **Lone-hand euchre scoring:** a few sources award 4 points to defenders who euchre a lone maker; the body uses the majority 2 points. (Pagat's summary ambiguously mentions 4.)
