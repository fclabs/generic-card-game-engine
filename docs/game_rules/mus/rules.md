# Mus

Mus is a Spanish/Basque partnership bluffing and betting game for four players with a 40-card Spanish deck. Players may discard and redraw cards, then bet on four "lances" (rounds): Grande, Chica, Pares and Juego/Punto. The first partnership to reach 40 points wins the game.

The main body follows the most common modern Spanish rule set ("8 reyes" / eight kings and eight aces). Differences are listed in Appendix A.

---

## 1. Components

- **Deck:** Spanish 40-card deck. Suits: Oros (coins), Copas (cups), Espadas (swords), Bastos (batons). Ranks: Rey (King, 12), Caballo (Horse, 11), Sota (Jack, 10), 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, As (Ace, 1).
- **Players:** 4, in two fixed partnerships. Partners sit opposite each other (seating alternates between the teams). Play and speaking order go to the right (counter-clockwise in most texts).
- **Counters (piedras/tantos/amarrakos):** Score is kept with stones. A 1-point stone is a *piedra*/*tanto*. A 5-point stone is an *amarraco* (*amarrako*). A game to 40 is 8 amarracos. The scorekeeper of each team holds the single stones, and the partner holds the 5-point stones.
- **Roles per hand:**
  - *Repartidor* (dealer).
  - *Mano*: the player to the dealer's right. Mano speaks first in every phase and wins ties.
  - *Postre*: the dealer, who speaks last.

## 2. Objective

Score 40 points (8 amarracos) before the opposing partnership. Points come from winning lances, from having Pares or Juego, and from bets (envites). A complete match is usually several games (see section 7).

## 3. Setup and dealing

1. The dealer is chosen by lot (for example, cut for the highest card). Each hand, the dealer role and mano rotate one seat to the right.
2. The dealer shuffles, the player to the dealer's left cuts, and the dealer deals **4 cards to each player, one at a time**, starting with the player to the dealer's right (mano) and ending with the dealer. The rest of the deck is the stock.
3. Players look at their own cards only.

## 4. Gameplay

### 4.1 Hand overview

1. Mus phase: discarding and redrawing (optional, needs unanimity).
2. Four lances, always in this order: **Grande, Chica, Pares, Juego** (or **Punto** if nobody has Juego).
3. Showdown and scoring.

### 4.2 Mus phase (descarte)

1. Starting with mano and going right, each player says **"Mus"** (I want to discard) or **"No hay mus"** / **"Paso"** (cut the mus).
2. If all four say Mus, discarding happens. If any player cuts the mus, discarding ends and betting starts at once. A player who cuts keeps the hand he holds.
3. Discard: starting with mano, each player discards 1 to 4 cards face down. The dealer deals the replacements from the stock, all at once for each player. Cards are never taken back.
4. After a discard round, the mus question is asked again, from mano. Discarding repeats until someone cuts the mus.
5. If the stock runs out, the discards are shuffled into a new stock. If only one player still needs cards, the new stock is the discards excluding that player's own discard.

### 4.3 The lances

Each lance is resolved in turn, with betting first and comparison afterwards. Within a lance, speaking starts with mano and moves right among the **eligible** players.

#### Grande (high)
- Card order, high to low: **Rey/3 (equal), Caballo, Sota, 7, 6, 5, 4, As/2 (equal)**.
- Compare the highest card of each hand. If equal, compare the second, third and fourth. The hand with the better first difference wins.

#### Chica (low)
- Same ranking, reversed. Compare the lowest card first, then the next lowest, and so on. The **lower** hand wins.

#### Pares (pairs)
- Before betting, each player in turn says "Pares sí" or "Pares no" (truthfully), starting with mano. A player with no pares cannot take part in the betting.
- Types, from lowest to highest:
  1. **Pares** (pareja): two cards of the same rank.
  2. **Medias**: three cards of the same rank.
  3. **Duples**: two pairs (four of a kind counts as duples).
- Any Duples beats any Medias, which beats any Pares. Same type: compare the rank (Rey/3 high, As/2 low). For Duples, compare the higher pair first, then the lower pair.
- With 8 reyes, a 3 counts as a Rey and a 2 as an As for pairs. A pair of 3s and a pair of Reyes are equal.
- **Skip rules:** If no one has Pares, the lance is skipped. If only one team has players with Pares, there is no betting and that team scores its Pares values (see 5.3).

#### Juego (game) and Punto
- **Card values for the count:** Rey, Caballo, Sota and 3 = 10 each (with 8 reyes); 7 = 7; 6 = 6; 5 = 5; 4 = 4; As and 2 = 1 each. A hand's total is the sum of its four cards.
- **Juego:** a total of **31 or more**.
- Before betting, each player in turn says "Juego sí" or "Juego no", starting with mano.
- Juego ranking, best to worst: **31, 32, 40, 37, 36, 35, 34, 33**. (Totals 38 and 39 are impossible.)
- If **no player** has Juego, the lance becomes **Punto**. All four players take part, and the best total of 30 or less wins: 30 best, then 29, 28, ... down to 4.
- If only one team has players with Juego, there is no betting and that team scores its Juego values.

#### Tie-break (all lances)
If two hands are equal for the lance, the winner is the player nearest to mano in speaking order (mano first, then rightward). Only the best single hand per team counts for the comparison.

### 4.4 Betting in a lance

Each lance with at least one eligible player on each team has a betting round. Betting is for the whole team: the best hand of the team represents it at the showdown.

**Actions:**
- **Paso**: pass, make no bet.
- **Envido**: bet. The default is 2 stones. A different amount may be named (minimum 2).
- **Envido más** / **Reenvido** / **Subo**: raise. Increase the standing bet by at least 2 (default 2).
- **Quiero** / **Veo**: accept the standing bet.
- **No quiero**: refuse the standing bet.
- **Órdago**: bet the entire game on this lance (see section 6).

**Flow:**
1. Players speak in turn, starting with mano. Eligible players are everyone in Grande and Chica, those with Pares in Pares, and those with Juego in Juego (everyone in Punto).
2. If everyone passes, nobody bet: the lance is "in paso" and is decided at the showdown for **1 stone** (plus Pares/Juego values where they apply).
3. After a player passes, a later player (a rival, or his own partner) may still bet.
4. When a bet or raise is made, the opposing team must answer: accept, refuse, or raise. Either opponent may answer for the team: if one says "quiero" and his partner "no quiero", the "quiero" stands. If both refuse, the bet is refused.
5. Raises can continue back and forth until someone accepts or refuses.
6. **Refusal:** The team that refuses concedes the lance. The bettor's team scores the **last accepted stake**: 1 stone if the refused bet was the first bet, otherwise the amount of the bet before the last raise. No comparison is made, and the stones are counted immediately.
7. **Acceptance:** The accepted amount is decided at the showdown. The lance winner's team scores the agreed amount.
8. After a lance is closed, the next lance starts, again with mano speaking first.

### 4.5 Showdown and scoring order

After the Juego/Punto lance, all hands are shown (a hand that is not needed for any score is still shown for verification). Points are then added in lance order: Grande, Chica, Pares, Juego/Punto. The score is added for:
- accepted bets (to the lance winner),
- lances played "in paso" (1 stone to the winner),
- Pares and Juego values (section 5.3).

The game ends as soon as a team reaches 40, even in the middle of this process. Stones from refused bets are scored immediately when the refusal happens (which can also end the game).

## 5. Special terms and scoring values

### 5.1 Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Mano | First player to speak (dealer's right); wins ties. |
| Postre | Last player (the dealer). |
| Lance | One round of the hand: Grande, Chica, Pares, Juego/Punto. |
| Grande / Chica | Highest / lowest hand. |
| Pares, Medias, Duples | Pair, three of a kind, two pairs. |
| Juego / Punto | 31+ total / closest to 30 when nobody has Juego. |
| Mus | Request to discard. |
| Corte de mus | A player refusing mus ("no hay mus"). |
| Paso | Pass. |
| Envite / Envido | Bet (minimum 2). |
| Quiero / No quiero | Accept / refuse. |
| Órdago | All-in bet for the whole game. |
| Negada | The 1 stone awarded when the first bet is refused (also used for penalties). |
| Seña | Allowed partner signal (see 5.4). |
| Piedra / Tanto | 1 point. |
| Amarraco | 5-point counter. |

### 5.2 Hand values by card (8 reyes)
- Grande/Chica: As=2 equal, Rey=3 equal.
- Count for Juego/Punto: Rey, 3, Caballo, Sota = 10; 7, 6, 5, 4 = face; As, 2 = 1.

### 5.3 Fixed points (on top of any bets)
Fixed points are awarded to the team that **wins** the lance, for **both** partners' hands (not only the winning hand).
- **Pares:** Pares = 1 each, Medias = 2 each, Duples = 3 each.
- **Juego:** a Juego of 31 = 3; any other Juego = 2.
- **Punto:** the winner scores 1.
- **Grande / Chica:** no fixed points (1 stone only if the lance is in paso).

When there is no betting because one team has no Pares/Juego, the team that has them scores the values of its players' hands. If Pares or Juego is refused by one team, the team that bet still scores the fixed values at the end of the hand. When the Pares bet is accepted, the winner takes the bet plus the fixed values of both partners.

### 5.4 Señas (signals)
Partners may exchange truthful signals about their hand. No other communication is allowed. Common set:
- Bite lower lip: two Reyes.
- Show tongue (front): two Ases.
- Mouth twisted sideways: Medias.
- Raise eyebrows: Duples.
- Wink: 31.
- Raise both shoulders: 30.
- Close both eyes (ciego): a bad hand with nothing.

## 6. Órdago

- An órdago is a bet of the whole game on a single lance. It may be made in any lance, as a bet or a raise.
- The opposing team may accept or refuse. If it **accepts**, the hands are shown at once, only that lance is compared, and the winning team **wins the game** (the lance is not scored with other lances).
- If it **refuses**, the team that made the órdago scores the refusal value (1 stone if it was the first bet, or the last accepted stake) and the hand continues.
- The accepting team shows its cards first.

## 7. Ending the game and the match

- A **game** is won by the first team to reach **40 stones (8 amarracos)**.
- A **match** (*vaca*) is usually the best of three games (first to win 2). Longer matches (best of five) are also common (Appendix A).
- Next game: the deal order continues; mano/dealer continues to rotate to the right.
- A team holding 7 amarracos (35 points) sometimes announces "dentro" to warn of the nearness of the win. This is a scorekeeping convention only.

## 8. Edge cases and clarifications

- **Mano wins ties** in every lance, including Punto, and among identical hands the player nearest to mano wins.
- **Equal hands with 3 = Rey and 2 = As:** these count as equal in Grande/Chica and Pares. Tied hands are decided by position, not suit.
- **Pares/Juego declarations must be truthful.** If a player forgets to declare Pares/Juego, he must correct it before the lance begins, or his hand cannot score.
- **Dealer replenishing the deck:** discards are reshuffled when the stock is exhausted.
- **Four cards after discard:** in a discard round each player must request at least one card (except in a "mus visto", see Appendix A).
- **Pares bets and the partner:** a team's Pares score counts both partners' pairs even if the partner with the better hand is not the one who bet.
- **No Pares/Juego for a team:** the lance is not contested; only the team that holds them scores.
- **Punto when nobody has Juego:** every player participates and the lance is bet like any other.
- **A 31 held by the mano:** it wins all ties with other 31s because mano wins ties.
- **A bet is not over until accepted or refused.** A player cannot back out of an accepted bet.
- **Showing cards during an órdago** commits the team to the bet.
- **Stakes may exceed 40.** Bet amounts can exceed the number of points left to win; the game still ends at 40.

## Appendix A: Rule Variations

Items marked **(differs)** change the main rules.

1. **4 reyes / 4 ases (Basque, Navarre, La Rioja) (differs):** 3s and 2s have their own rank and value. Card order: Rey, Caballo, Sota, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, As (Grande high to Chica low). Count: 3 = 3, 2 = 2, As = 1. Some sources pair this with a 40-point game and the 8 reyes version with 30 points (see item 2).
2. **Game length (differs):** Most sources play to 40 stones (8 amarracos). Some regions and sources (for example some Basque texts) use 30 stones (6 amarracos), often with the 8 reyes version.
3. **Match length (differs):** Pagat: best of three games. Spanish tournament rules: first to win 3 games of 40. Other sources: a "vaca" of 3, 5 or 7 games. In tournaments, a decider game may be shortened to 20.
4. **31 real / Juego real (differs):** Three 7s plus one 10-point card (some require the Sota of Oros; others any sota) = 31 and beats an ordinary 31, even one held by mano. Counts as 3 points. Not used in some tournaments.
5. **Mus corrido (differs):** In some rules the first hand of a game, or any hand, is "corrida": mus may be cut by any player at any time, and the player who cuts becomes mano. In others "mus corrido" is played only if the mano wants. Mus corto (a single round of discards) is mentioned in some sources, but the sources consulted did not define it uniformly, so it is not in the main rules.
6. **Mus visto (differs):** If a card is accidentally exposed while dealing, a "mus visto" takes place. Mano alone decides whether there is mus; if there is, any of the other players may stay "servido" (keep his 4 cards) without discarding, and then there is no further mus.
7. **Discard order (differs):** Pagat and most texts start the discard with mano. Spanish tournament rules start with the dealer and continue to the left.
8. **Perete / 4-5-6-7 (differs):** A player dealt 4, 5, 6, 7 may show them and get a new hand before the mus (a "perete"). Some tournaments forbid this.
9. **Deje (differs):** In some tournaments, Pares/Juego/Punto are played "con deje": an extra stone is scored when a team refuses a previous bet. The sources do not give a precise calculation, so the main rule (last accepted stake) is used.
10. **Ten kings (differs):** The Sota de Copas and Sota de Oros are jokers that count as As or Rey, chosen at showdown.
11. **Six players (differs):** Two teams of three, seated alternately; same scoring; sometimes to 50. A captain may decide the mus and bets.
12. **Señas (differs):** Signals vary. Some groups also use shoulder raise for 29 (one shoulder) or 30 (both shoulders), tongue to the side for medias of aces, and closed lips pushed out for any Juego. False or unauthorized signs are penalised in tournaments (typically loss of the lance or the game).
13. **Penalties (differs):** Tournament rules score a "negada" (1) for the opponents on procedural errors (wrong declarations, showing cards early, accepting an órdago at Juego with the worst Juego (33) as postre, etc.). These are not needed for a standard digital implementation.
14. **Dentro:** Pagat describes announcing "dentro" at 35 points, which is a scorekeeping habit only.

## Appendix B: Notes on conflicting sources

- Fixed Pares/Juego values (1/2/3 and 3/2/1) agree across all sources.
- Some secondary web summaries claim "30 points per game" or "3 games = 1 vaca" as universal; the more detailed sources (Pagat, Spanish tournament regulations) use 40 points, so 40 was chosen as the main rule.
- The rule that a refused bet gives the "last accepted stake" (1 for the first bet) follows Pagat and the tournament regulations.
